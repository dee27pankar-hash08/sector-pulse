    Methodology, sources & limitations

    Data sources (all free and official, refreshed daily)

    AMFI (Association of Mutual Funds in India): the official daily NAV file for every scheme. It powers the scheme search and latest NAVs.

    NAV history: full daily NAV history per scheme from api.mfapi.in, a public mirror of AMFI's historical NAV data.

    US Treasury: daily par yield curve rates (3M, 2Y, 10Y).

    ECB reference rates (via Frankfurter): daily USD/INR.

    Server responses are cached for up to 6 hours. NAVs are published once per business day, so figures reflect the latest published NAV date shown at the top.

    Why sector index funds instead of index levels?

    NSE's own index feeds block automated access from cloud servers. Instead, each sector is measured through the longest-running passive fund or ETF tracking that exact Nifty sector index, using AMFI's official NAVs. These track the index closely; the gap is roughly the fund's expense ratio (typically 0.1–0.5% a year) plus small tracking error. Each row shows the proxy used. Unit splits in ETF NAV histories are detected and adjusted automatically.

    Market Pulse definitions

    Returns: point-to-point NAV change over 7, 30, 91, 182 and 365 calendar days; YTD from the last NAV of the previous year. 3Y CAGR: (end ÷ start)^(1/3) − 1.

    RS 3M (relative strength): sector 3-month return minus Nifty 50 3-month return. Breadth: share of sectors trading above their 200-day moving average. Risk appetite: average of Midcap 150 and Smallcap 250 3-month returns minus Nifty 50 (above +2pp = risk-on, below −2pp = risk-off).

    Trend: Uptrend if price > 50-DMA > 200-DMA; Downtrend if price < 50-DMA < 200-DMA; otherwise Mixed. Vol 1Y: annualised standard deviation of daily returns. Max DD 1Y: largest peak-to-trough fall in the last year.

    Fund due-diligence definitions (window: last 3 years, or full history if shorter)

    Sharpe = (CAGR − risk-free) ÷ annualised volatility. Sortino uses downside deviation below the risk-free rate. Beta = cov(fund, bench) ÷ var(bench) on daily returns. Jensen's alpha = CAGR − [rf + β × (bench CAGR − rf)].

    Tracking error = annualised stdev of daily (fund − bench) returns. Information ratio = (fund CAGR − bench CAGR) ÷ tracking error.

    Up / down capture: geometric average fund return in months when the benchmark rose (fell), divided by the benchmark's. Ideal: up > 100%, down < 100%. Batting average: share of months the fund beat the benchmark.

    Rolling returns: 3-year CAGR measured from every week in the history (1-year if under 4 years of data). This removes the end-point bias of a single trailing return.

    Flags and verdict: rule-based thresholds (e.g. rolling beat rate under 40% = red, information ratio under 0 = red, down capture over 110% = red). Two or more reds = "Review for replacement"; one red or two ambers = "Watchlist"; otherwise "Retain". This is only the quantitative screen. A real review also covers the 4 Ps and operational due diligence.

    Limitations

    Benchmarks are price-return proxies via passive funds, not official TRI series, so they carry a small expense drag that slightly flatters active funds. Multi-cap and flexi-cap funds would ideally be measured against the Nifty 500 TRI; choose the closest benchmark manually. Past performance does not guarantee future results.
